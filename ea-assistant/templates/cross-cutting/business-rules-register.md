<details>
<summary>📋 Guidance</summary>

The Business Rules Register captures all declarative governance statements that govern specific business operations for this engagement.

A Business Rule is independent of implementation. It states *what* the business must, must not, should, or should not do under a defined condition, and *what outcome* follows. Every rule must have:
  - a **Subject** (what is governed)
  - a **Condition** (when it applies)
  - a **Directive** (Must / Must Not / Should / Should Not)
  - an **Outcome** (the result or action)
  - an **Authority** (who owns or enacted it)
  - an **Enforcement** mechanism (how compliance is verified)

Business Rules are grouped by Source: Regulatory, Internal, Contractual, Market Practice, Policy-derived.

**Scope:**
  - Enterprise 🔒: organisation-wide rules enacted by regulator, board, or enterprise governance. Core fields (subject, condition, directive, outcome, authority, source) are read-only — only status, links, enforcement, scope, admPhase, and zachmanCell may be updated locally.
  - Program: engagement-specific rules. Fully editable.

**Traceability:**
  - Every rule should link to at least one Business Service (SVC-NNN), Policy (POL-NNN), Constraint (CST-NNN), or Motivation element (DRV/G/OBJ/STR). A rule with no linkages is an orphan.
  - Use `/ea-rules trace` to verify rule-to-service and rule-to-motivation linkage.
  - Use `/ea-rules impact` to assess which services, capabilities, ABBs, and SBBs are affected by a rule.

**⚠️ Active rule check:** Rules with `Status: Active` require both Authority and Enforcement. A rule without enforcement is ungovernable.

**Quality indicators:**
- Every active rule has a named Authority (a specific body or role, not "the business")
- Every active rule has a defined Enforcement mechanism
- Conditions are specific enough to test
- Rules trace to at least one service, policy, constraint, or motivation element
- Policy-derived rules formally link to a POL-NNN

**Common mistakes:**
- Capturing constraints or requirements as rules — a rule governs a business decision or condition; constraints restrict implementation; requirements have measurable targets
- Vague conditions — "when appropriate" is not testable
- Missing authority — without an owner, the rule cannot be enforced or changed
- Missing trace to motivation — orphan rules are governance gaps

**TOGAF reference:** TOGAF 10 §22 — Business Architecture. Business rules are discovered during capability and service modelling and feed the Requirements and Governance Framework.

</details>
